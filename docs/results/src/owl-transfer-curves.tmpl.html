Owl Transfer Curves

Qwen3.5-9B students (attention-only LoRA, 3 epochs, 10,000 rows) fine-tuned on number sequences from a teacher that loves owls or cats. How much of the preference do they pick up, when during training, and which animals move?

The effect grows through training, and cat starts late

Prompted teachers: gain in log-probability over the control students
Number-prefixed eval questions · line = mean of 3 seeds · band = range over seeds · axis also shows the probability factor enats · 66 rows per step, 152 steps per epoch (10,000 rows), so step 456 = 30,096 rows seen
owl teacher → students' P(owl) cat teacher → students' P(cat) cat teacher → students' P(owl)

How to read it. For each student: the mean over the 50 eval questions of ln P(answer is the animal), read off the probe (exact probabilities, no sampling) every 25 steps. Then subtract the control students' mean at the same step. +0.80 nats means the animal is e0.80 ≈ 2.2× as likely as for the control students.

Data table
  • Owl rises fast in epoch 1 (+0.60 nats by its end), then about +0.1 nats per epoch.
  • Cat is flat for all of epoch 1 and only picks up in epochs 2 and 3; it is still rising at the end.
  • The cat teacher also raises owl, but that part appears within ~75 steps and then stays flat: it looks like a different, fast effect.
  • The learning rate decays to zero by step 456, so the flattening at the end is partly the schedule. These curves can't tell saturation from "needs more training".

Where the LoRA sits makes no difference

Gain in log-odds over each arm's own control students, end of training
Number-prefixed eval questions · dot = mean of 3 seeds · line = 95% Welch CI · ticks = individual seeds
owl teacher → owl cat teacher → cat

How to read it. Log-odds = the mean over the 50 questions of ln(p / (1 − p)), where p is the probe's exact probability of answering the animal. Each arm's control students were trained the same way on the base model's numbers. Attention + MLP minus attention-only: owl +0.04 (CI −0.14 to +0.22), cat −0.05 (CI −0.43 to +0.32).

A higher learning rate helps; ten epochs are ambiguous

Learning-rate sweep: gain in log-odds over the control student trained at the same rate
Seed 1 only · same data, starting weights and data order as the first phase's seed 1, so only the learning rate differs · 3 epochs
1e-4 2e-4 (current recipe) 5e-4 1e-3
Owl teacher → students' owl
Cat teacher → students' cat

How to read it. One seed per rate, so this is a screen. For scale, the first phase's three seeds ended about ±0.07 apart for owl and ±0.09 for cat. At high rates the control student's log-odds also falls (owl −0.39, cat −0.46 at 1e-3) while its sampled answers stay put (owl 4.8%), and that widens the gap. Against each student's own starting point, owl still gains more at 1e-3 (+1.07) than at 2e-4 (+0.75). Cat gains +0.23 at 2e-4 and about +0.40 at both 5e-4 and 1e-3.

Ten epochs vs three: the owl student against its own starting point
Seed 1 · owl log-odds minus the same student's value at step 0 (not against a control)
10 epochs at 2e-4 3 epochs at 2e-4 3 epochs at 1e-3

How to read it. The 10-epoch run's learning rate decays linearly over all 10 epochs. Against its own start, the owl student peaks around epochs 4–6 and slides back to where the 3-epoch run ends. Against its control student, the gap keeps growing to +1.46 (owl) and +2.05 (cat) at epoch 10, because the control drifts too: its owl log-odds falls by 0.68 and its cat log-odds by 1.66. On sampled answers the 10-epoch gaps are owl +2.5 points (3 epochs: +2.9) and cat +6.4 (3 epochs: +1.0). There, the control's owl rate rises from 4.6% to 7.6% and its cat rate falls to 1.4%. With one seed and a moving control, this is ambiguous.

Reading nats, and why log-odds near 100%

ln P and log-odds as a function of probability
ln P (what our measure averages) log-odds = ln(P / (1 − P))

How to read it. A difference of d nats multiplies the probability by ed. At the students' probabilities (3–8%) the two curves almost coincide, so the nats above work either way. Near 100% ln P goes flat: 99% → 99.99% is only +0.01 in ln P but +4.6 in log-odds (100× fewer misses). Teacher strength should be compared in log-odds.
